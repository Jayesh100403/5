About

From transparent electrodes to the physics of light.

My thesis began as a photovoltaic-electrode project. The part that stayed with me most was not the device label, but the optical physics behind it.

I became increasingly interested in how layer thickness, interfaces, wavelength-dependent optical constants and nanoscale morphology reshape the propagation of light. That shift led me to focus my M.Sc. work on optical design and light management rather than treating simulation only as a device-optimization tool.

I value experimentally grounded modelling. Ideal simulations can reveal mechanisms and design limits, but fabrication determines what is physically achievable. Roughness, discontinuity, non-uniformity and interface quality are not afterthoughts; they are part of the optical problem.
